Sat, Sep 19, 11:00 AM · 1u · injury

Clemson starts true freshman Tait Reynolds (Vizzina week-to-week, elbow) in his first career start against a Belichick defense that has allowed 13 points in two games (10 to TCU, 3 to ETSU). Clemson's offense managed 10 at LSU and 22 against Georgia Southern with the veteran; a first-start dual-threat freshman means a run-heavy, clock-eating plan. UNC's own offense scored 15 against TCU. The spread moved six points on the QB news but the total moved only one, from 44.5 to 43.5; that is the mismatch.

Bear case: Low college totals are dangerous: one kick-return touchdown or a couple of short fields from freshman turnovers gets to 44 without either offense doing anything. UNC hung 35 on ETSU, Clemson has the better athletes and could pull away late, and the number is already the lowest of the week's ACC games so the market is not asleep.

CollegeMISS +3 (-108)LSU @ MISS 5/10
Sat, Sep 19, 6:30 PM · 1u · motivation

Lane Kiffin returns to Oxford as LSU's coach and the line has moved 4.5 points toward LSU, from Ole Miss -1.5 to LSU -3, without a reported Ole Miss injury to explain it. That gives us the home dog at +3, on the key number, in a night game that will be the most hostile environment of the college season. Ole Miss is 2-0 with Trinidad Chambliss, who had 385 total yards against this same Blake Baker defense last year, and LSU's right tackle Weston Davis left last week's game with an injury on an offensive line its own beat writers say must improve significantly.

Bear case: The move toward LSU may be sharp: LSU has looked like the better team (51-10 over Clemson) and Ole Miss's offensive line has been battling injuries of its own since the Louisville game. Emotional revenge spots are a narrative, not an edge, and Ole Miss's roster turned over heavily after Kiffin left. If LSU is simply 5 points better, +3 is not enough.

NFLJAX +2.5 (-105)JAX @ DEN 5/10
Sun, Sep 20, 3:05 PM · 1u · situational

Denver plays Monday night at Kansas City, loses 31-10, and then hosts a rested Jacksonville team six days later. Short weeks after a road prime-time loss are a classic fade spot, and the market is still pricing Denver as a 58% favorite while ESPN's FPI makes Jacksonville a 63% favorite: a rare 20-point disagreement with no injury explaining it (Denver had no injury designations for week 1). Jacksonville beat Cleveland 34-10 and Brian Thomas Jr. is expected to play.

Bear case: Mile High is a real home-field advantage, Jacksonville has to play at altitude in a 3:05 kickoff, and Denver's 31-10 loss came at Kansas City where everyone loses. FPI is one model and the market already knows it. Thomas Jr. is limited all week with the shoulder; if he is a decoy the Jaguars passing game loses its explosive element. This is a thin, mostly situational edge.

NFLNYG +7 (-105)NYG @ LAR 5/10
Mon, Sep 21, 7:15 PM · 1u · injury

The Rams put Myles Garrett on IR Monday (knee surgery, out through week 5) and the line only moved from -8.5 to -7. Losing the best pass rusher in football is worth more than 1.5 points against an offense that just beat Dallas, and the number lands on 7, the second-most common NFL margin, so we are on the right side of the key number. The Rams are also coming off a 27-7 no-show against SF in Australia, with a body clock reset before a Monday night game.

Bear case: Los Angeles is at home with 10 days of rest, Stafford and McVay against a Giants team that is 1-0 largely because Dallas turned it over; Aaron Donald may return, which would offset part of the Garrett loss; and the Giants are traveling cross-country after their own Monday night game with a depleted secondary (three corners on IR). A 7-point favorite at home that is simply the better team covers plenty.
